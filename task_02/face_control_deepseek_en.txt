TECHNOLOGY FOR IDENTITY VERIFICATION OF A PERSON FOR MOBILE DEVICES USING AN IDENTITY DOCUMENT AND A FACE IMAGE IN A SINGLE IMAGE

Client identity verification by selfie and photograph on an identity document is actively used in airports, banks, and government institutions to speed up service and reduce the human factor. However, to date there is no unified technology on the basis of which automatic matching of a face and its image on a document from a single frame in a closed loop is performed. Due to data protection requirements, the process is increasingly transferred to user mobile devices, which imposes restrictions on computational resources. The article describes a technology for matching a face and its image on a Russian Federation passport from a single photograph. This technology was implemented as the FaceDocVerify system, suitable for use even on mobile devices. Validation on a closed data corpus revealed no type I errors, while type II errors are present in isolated cases and have a trivial explanation. The average processing time by FaceDocVerify of one frame on iPhone 17, iPhone 14 Pro Max, and Nothing Phone 3a was 183 ms, 291 ms, and 872 ms, respectively.

Introduction

Face image recognition technologies are used in many areas: for identity verification at border control, when paying for purchases using a facial profile, and in head pose estimation. In these areas, the cost of an error is extremely high, which determines special attention to the accuracy of these methods.

This work is devoted to systems based on face image recognition technologies and used for verifying the identity of a bearer, which is performed by comparing the facial image of the document holder with his/her photograph on an identity document. Such systems have become especially relevant due to the widespread adoption of remote identity confirmation when issuing credit or providing other banking services using mobile devices. At the same time, image processing related to biometric data, which includes the bearer’s face, must be carried out directly on the mobile device itself, bypassing the transmission of sensitive data outside it—otherwise there is a risk of disclosing the user’s confidential information. The computational capabilities of modern smartphones are lower than those of personal computers, and even more so server systems. For this reason, for methods and algorithms intended for mobile devices, in addition to accuracy, strict restrictions on computational resources apply.

In the considered formulation, the identity verification task has features that create significant difficulties: a substantial difference in the geometric parameters of the face in the selfie and on the document; a difference in shooting conditions; the presence of glare on the document; differences in a person’s appearance caused, for example, by age-related changes, the presence of makeup or a beard; and differences in accessories.

The main obstacle in solving this task is the deficit of public annotated data for training and testing models. This is due to two main reasons. First, the confidentiality of details in the identity document and in the facial image. Second, the task formulation often requires that a single image in the dataset contain both the bearer and his/her document. Such public data corpora currently do not exist.

This work describes an implementation in the form of a unique system that allows automatic extraction and comparison of the facial images of the bearer and the face extracted from an image of a Russian Federation passport for the purpose of identity verification. The architecture of such a system is presented, the modules and models used in it are described, as well as the methodology for training an artificial neural network for comparing facial images. The implementation of the FaceDocVerify system is considered, for which validation is carried out on a closed data corpus built on the basis of a set of samples from volunteers.

This work consists of four chapters. Chapter 1 considers existing approaches to face verification. Chapter 2 is devoted to describing the architecture and components of the proposed FaceDocVerify technology. Chapter 3 presents the architecture of a specialized artificial neural network responsible for face comparison. Chapter 4 contains a description of the experiments conducted and an analysis of the results obtained.

1. Literature review

The subtask of face verification from the field of face image recognition technologies in a broad sense means checking whether two facial images belong to one person. Many publications are devoted to its solution. This work considers a narrower formulation of this task, namely comparing the face of a document holder and his/her face in an image of an identity document. Such an extremely important scenario arises in banking when it is necessary to confirm a client’s identity by his/her face and an image of a Russian Federation passport. In this case, the images may differ substantially, and the application of general face verification methods shows insufficient accuracy.

One of the first works concerning the face verification task is an article published in 2000. The authors apply a classical approach including eye localization using the Hough transform, cropping images while preserving the most invariant parts of the face, and transforming the image into a rank map of edges. The same authors additionally consider face verification using a geometric method and a three-layer perceptron.

Due to the growth of computational power, deep learning methods began to dominate in face recognition technologies. For face comparison, methods based on metric learning have proven themselves very well. One of the key developments in the field of face verification for matching a document and a photograph is the DocFace method and its successor DocFace+. These methods use an architecture of two parallel branches based on a convolutional neural network with residual blocks, pretrained on a large public dataset with facial images. One branch is trained on bearer photographs, and the second on document photographs. For consistency of the feature space, the branches use shared high-level parameters.

In one work, a comparison of face verification methods is conducted and an own solution to this task is proposed, based on fine-tuning and adjusting a model with the ResNet-100 architecture and the ArcFace loss function.

The main difficulty in solving the face verification task is the absence of publicly available datasets for training and testing artificial neural networks, which is primarily due to legal restrictions. Thus, in the Russian Federation, the distribution of Russian passport data is regulated by Federal Law “On Personal Data” No. 152-FZ, which turns the publication of a corresponding data corpus into a nontrivial task, primarily from a legal point of view.

Let us consider two works by foreign scientists. In the first of them, the pattern of insufficient training data is highlighted. The authors collect their own data corpus with Brazilian documents containing 34,999 pairs of “person photograph — document photograph.” To great regret, it is unavailable to other researchers. In the second work, the authors also collected their own data corpus, Chilean Young Adult, containing 263,490 pairs with photographs of Chilean adolescents aged 18–19 and their documents issued at age 9–18.

A common limitation of the considered works is their orientation toward regional types of documents, which does not allow the proposed verification methods to be directly transferred to documents of other countries or the presented data corpora to be used for training. The solutions considered above rely on the fact that the photographs of the document and its bearer are read separately, that is, the situation when the document and the bearer are depicted in one photograph is ignored by them. Determining the document type is a separate nontrivial task, and a system for solving it must satisfy the following requirements: ensuring operation without transmitting data via the Internet and using cloud storage, operation on mobile devices and under different lighting conditions, and performing non-biometric face matching in photographs.

At the same time, there are two formats of input data: separate photographs of the document and the bearer’s face, or a single frame in which the bearer holds the document in his/her hands. This work considers precisely the latter option.

For the development of methods for automatic processing of identity documents, the emergence of the MIDV family of data corpora became a breakthrough, including images of documents whose contents are synthesized according to templates and the structure of document details. However, in the case of the face verification task considered in this work, additional difficulties arise. First, data with Russian passports are required. Second, each data sample must contain, in one image, both the document itself and the face of its holder.

Under conditions of a shortage of training and test data, methods for synthesizing images of “live” faces and the corresponding faces on identity documents are being actively researched. In one work, an approach based on using a generative model to transfer faces from bearer photographs onto document images is proposed. Such an approach has a significant drawback: the need to have a large corpus of high-resolution images for training the artificial neural network. Under such conditions, an obvious approach for obtaining a sufficient amount of training and test data is to independently create a data corpus.

2. Description of the proposed technology

The implementation of the proposed technology in the form of the FaceDocVerify system has a modular structure. The input to the system is a digital image registered by the bearer using a mobile device camera. It is assumed that this image contains the bearer’s image with the second and third pages of a Russian Federation passport spread out. In other words, the photograph presents two faces. It is required to automatically check the hypothesis that these faces belong to one bearer. At the output, the system forms a verdict taking one of three values: Correct — the hypothesis is confirmed, Incorrect — it is not confirmed, Undefined — rendering a verdict is impossible.

First, a module is launched on the image that performs localization and processing of the Russian Federation passport. If the passport is not found in the image, the system verdict will be Undefined. Otherwise, the system has access to information about the passport’s position in the scene and, thereby, can establish an a priori position of the facial image on the document.

After localizing the passport, a face search module is launched, which returns a set of detected faces. Among the detected faces, the main face is selected, that is, the face outside the document with the largest area. If such a face is not found, the system result will be the verdict Undefined.

If the presented document type corresponds to a Russian Federation passport, the location of the face on the document is localized based on relative coordinates. If the face on the document is noticeably occluded by glare, blurred, or has other significant defects, the system stops operation with the verdict Undefined.

Additionally, the ratio of the areas of the quadrilaterals of the face on the passport to the area of the main face is calculated. If this ratio is sufficiently high and close to one, a conclusion is made that the objects in the image are incorrect, and the system terminates its operation on this frame with the verdict Undefined.

Next, the facial images are fed to the input of the image normalization module. Normalization of facial images allows unifying the cropped regions of the frame, which have different sizes, shooting conditions, and distortions. All image pixels are converted to grayscale.

At the next step, the normalized images of the two faces are transmitted to the input of the face comparison module. This module, using an artificial neural network, compares two images and returns a similarity characteristic, which reflects the network’s confidence that the faces belong to one person. If the similarity characteristic exceeds a predetermined threshold value, verification is considered successfully passed, and the system issues the verdict Correct. Otherwise, the system issues the verdict Incorrect, which means the images belong to different people.

3. Facial image comparison module

3.1. Architecture of the artificial neural network

To compare facial images in the FaceDocVerify system, a special artificial neural network is used. Its architecture is a sequence of blocks consisting of a convolutional layer, a batch normalization layer, and an activation function. The network takes as input a normalized grayscale facial image of size 56 by 56 pixels, and at the output produces a hidden representation of the face—a vector of 60 real floating-point numbers with a total length of 240 bytes. The resulting hidden representations are compared with each other by Euclidean norm. Then the resulting distance is normalized to the range from 0 to 1 using a piecewise linear function.

The number of trainable network parameters is 1.4 × 10⁶. During the forward pass of the network, about 5.4 × 10⁷ elementary arithmetic operations are performed. The total model weight is 5.1 megabytes. These characteristics are especially important because the system is aimed at functioning in a closed loop of mobile devices without access to powerful computational resources.

During network training, a composite metric loss function presented in a recent work was used. It includes three components: a triplet loss function; the proximity of positive pairs in each triplet to increase the compactness of intra-class representations; and a regularization component that minimizes the distance between the hidden representation of a face and the mean vector of the corresponding class, and also increases the distance between negative examples of the same class.

To balance the contribution of the loss function components, normalization of the gradients of each term to a fixed value was used, which made it possible to avoid domination by one of the components during optimization. The values of the coefficients were selected empirically.

3.2. Training data and augmentations

The training sample contains about six million images, accounting for about one million classes, that is, unique individuals. This sample is built on the basis of the open data corpora VGG Face, MegaFace, as well as private data. The data include facial images of various natures, including synthesized samples, stylized and drawn using Arc2Face. Including such data makes it possible to increase the model’s generalization ability on nonstandard examples. It is important that when forming positive pairs, that is, belonging to one class, classes consisting of a single image were also used.

During network training, data augmentation methods were actively used, which is especially important to compensate for the small number of examples per class. Among them were typical transformations used in classical computer vision tasks: geometric—random rotations, horizontal reflections, scaling; filters and distortions—Gaussian filters, addition of additive normal noise, lines to imitate defects and artifacts on documents; color and brightness transformations—autocontrasting of the entire image and its individual parts.

Methods specific to face recognition technologies were also used, in particular imitation of head rotations. Unlike an ordinary rotation in the image plane, this transformation models perspective distortion that occurs when the head position changes relative to the camera. Its use makes it possible to increase the model’s robustness to changes in head pose, which are one of the key causes of degradation in face recognition quality in real conditions. This augmentation is especially important in cases of a small number of images per class, when the model is forced to generalize to previously unobserved viewing angles.

4. Experimental part

To evaluate the accuracy of the proposed method, a closed test set was collected, consisting of 238 photographs in which employees of Smart Engines Service LLC photograph themselves and simultaneously hold their own passport with the second and third pages spread perpendicular to the camera. The employees gave consent to such a variant of using images of their passports and their faces. These photographs were used to determine how well the system matches facial images of the same bearer. In addition, the set contains photographs in which bearers hold someone else’s passport, as well as photographs without the document bearer in the frame, with the bearer’s face occluded, and with a photograph on the document significantly occluded by glare.

Thus, the elements of the data corpus are pairs of the form “image — answer.” In total, the corpus contains 148 images in which the bearer’s face coincides with the face on the document, 83 images in which the faces do not coincide, and 7 images in which the faces are not subject to verification.

The FaceDocVerify system was run on the data corpus. The resulting multiclass confusion matrix was constructed. The FaceDocVerify system worked correctly for Incorrect and Undefined cases. However, the system makes type II errors. Analysis of the images showed that 66.67% of incorrect verdicts were given by the system on truly ambiguous images. For example, in one image the photograph on the passport was taken more than 20 years ago and looked different from the bearer’s photograph. In some images there were glare partially occluding the faces on the passport, which hindered comparison. In one image, the face on the passport and the bearer’s face differ so much that even a thorough visual assessment did not allow them to be correctly identified.

To measure the operating time of the FaceDocVerify system, mobile devices iPhone 17, iPhone 14 Pro Max, and Nothing Phone 3a were used. When processing images, the average analysis time of one frame on iPhone 17 was 183 ms, on iPhone 14 Pro Max — 291 ms, on Nothing Phone 3a — 872 ms. At the same time, the resolution of the input image for iOS devices is 2160 by 3840 pixels, for the Android device — 1256 by 2560 pixels. The indicated time includes the full processing cycle of the Russian Federation passport, including localization and recognition of details, as well as all processing of facial images, taking into account detection, selection of a pair of faces for comparison, and their direct matching.

Conclusion

This work presents a technology for human identity verification on mobile devices using an identity document and a facial image in one image in the form of the FaceDocVerify system. This system is capable of verifying a person by comparing his/her selfie and the face on a Russian Federation passport using only one frame. The FaceDocVerify system is not subject to “leakage” of confidential data, since all computational processes occur inside the user’s mobile device.

Due to the high accuracy of identity verification and complete isolation of the processed data inside the mobile device, the proposed FaceDocVerify system has been successfully integrated into the SmartIdEngine document recognition system.

The FaceDocVerify system is currently focused on identity verification using a Russian Federation passport. At the same time, scaling the proposed system to other types of identity documents containing the owner’s photograph is not substantially difficult. The only condition for solving this task is the availability of relevant data corpora in sufficient quantity to achieve the required classification accuracy.
